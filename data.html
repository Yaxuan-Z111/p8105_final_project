Variable Descriptions

SEX_A

Original Meaning

SEX_A identifies the biological sex of the respondent (1 = Male, 2 = Female) as defined in the NHIS adult codebook.

Recoding

The variable was recoded into a binary indicator where:

  • 1 = Male
  • 0 = Female

AGEP_A

Original Meaning

AGEP_A represents the respondent’s age in years.

Recoding

No recoding was performed; the variable is used as a continuous numeric measure.

DIBEV_A

Original Meaning

Indicates whether the respondent has ever been told by a doctor that they have diabetes (1 = Yes, 2 = No).

Recoding

The variable was converted to:

  • 1 = Diabetes
  • 0 = No diabetes
  • Responses 7, 8, and 9 (refused/don’t know/not ascertained) were removed.

BMICATD_A

Original Meaning

BMI category assigned based on measured height and weight:
1 = Underweight
2 = Healthy weight
3 = Overweight
4–6 = Obesity classes I–III

Recoding

BMI was regrouped into four meaningful analytic categories:

  • 0 = Healthy weight
  • 1 = Underweight
  • 2 = Overweight
  • 3 = Obesity (classes I–III combined)

WEIGHTLBTC_A

Original Meaning

Measured body weight in pounds.

HEIGHTTC_A

Original Meaning

Measured height in inches.

DIFF_A

Original Meaning

Self-reported difficulty walking or climbing steps (1 = None, 2 = Some, 3 = A lot, 4 = Cannot do).

Recoding

Recoded as:

  • 0 = No difficulty
  • 1 = Some difficulty
  • 2 = A lot of difficulty
  • 3 = Cannot do

EDUCP_A

Original Meaning

Highest educational attainment (ranging from no schooling to graduate degree).

Recoding

Grouped as:

  • 0 = Low education
  • 1 = Medium education
  • 2 = High education

LSATIS4_A

Original Meaning

Life satisfaction rating (1 = Very satisfied → 4 = Very dissatisfied).

Recoding

Combined into:

  • 0 = Satisfied (1–2)
  • 1 = Dissatisfied (3–4)

PHSTAT_A

Original Meaning

General health status (Excellent / Very good / Good / Fair / Poor).

Recoding

Reclassified into:

  • 0 = Good health (Excellent–Good)
  • 1 = Poor health (Fair/Poor)

CHLEV_A

Original Meaning

History of high cholesterol (1 = Yes, 2 = No).

Recoding

Converted to:

  • 1 = Yes
  • 0 = No

HYPEV_A

Original Meaning

History of hypertension.

Recoding

  • 1 = Yes
  • 0 = No.

LIVEREV_A

Original Meaning

History of liver disease.

Recoding

  • 1 = Yes
  • 0 = No.

STREV_A

Original Meaning

History of stroke.

Recoding

  • 1 = Yes
  • 0 = No.

MIEV_A

Original Meaning

History of myocardial infarction (heart attack).

Recoding

  • 1 = Yes
  • 0 = No.

ANGEV_A

Original Meaning

History of angina.

Recoding

  • 1 = Yes
  • 0 = No.

CHDEV_A

Original Meaning

History of coronary heart disease.

Recoding

  • 1 = Yes
  • 0 = No.

DEPEV_A

Original Meaning

Ever told had depression.

Recoding

  • 1 = Yes
  • 0 = No.

ANXEV_A

Original Meaning

Ever told had anxiety.

Recoding

  • 1 = Yes
  • 0 = No.

SMKEV_A

Original Meaning

Smoking history (1 = Yes, 2 = No).

Recoding

Binary coding: - 1 = Yes (Ever smoked) - 0 = No (Never smoked)

LONELY_A

Frequency of loneliness (1 = Always → 5 = Never).

Correlation Analysis

To better understand the relationships between the variables in our dataset, we find the need to conduct a correlation analysis. The original dataset was obtained from the 2024 NHIS (National Health Interview Survey), which contains information on demographic characteristics, health conditions, and behavioral factors for a nationally representative sample of adults. The cleaned dataset included variables such as age, sex, weight, height, and various health indicators.

Correlation analysis serves as a preliminary step to identify variables that are linearly associated. Understanding these relationships helps us: - Detect potential multicollinearity before performing regression analyses or more complex modeling. - Provide evidence-based justification for including or excluding certain variables in our following statistical models.

We computed pairwise Pearson correlations for all numeric variables. Correlations with an absolute value greater than 0.3 are considered moderate or strong associations. Additionally, diagonal elements (self-correlations) were excluded from interpretation, and symmetric duplicates were removed to ensure clarity.

To understand Coefficient of determination (\(R^2\)), \(R^2\) represents the proportion of the variance in one variable that is predictable from the other variable. For our selected threshold of \(r = 0.3\): \[R^2 = (0.3)^2 = 0.09\] This means that for two variables correlated at \(r = 0.3\), 9% of the variation in one variable is explained by the other variable.
